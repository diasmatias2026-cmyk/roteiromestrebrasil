## Narração limpa (para colar na voz)
That "tum-tum" your car makes on a bridge isn't a flaw. You've felt it a thousand times, right where the bridge begins. That gap is what keeps the bridge from bending out of shape. And the reason starts with the sun. Steel grows when it heats up. About twelve millionths of its length for every degree. Tiny. Until the steel is a hundred meters long. From a freezing winter to a forty-degree summer, the deck can grow almost five centimeters. Now lock both ends, and watch what happens. The steel has nowhere to go, so it pushes and bends. Railways see it every heat wave. They call it a sun kink. So engineers do something that sounds crazy. They cut the deck into pieces and join them with steel teeth, like two combs. In summer the teeth slide together. In winter, they pull apart. Even the giants do it. On a hot day, Sydney Harbour Bridge's arch can rise eighteen centimeters. So that "tum-tum" isn't a flaw. It's the bridge breathing. Follow for more.
